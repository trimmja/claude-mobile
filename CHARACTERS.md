# JBE — Character Bios

Living design reference. Update this whenever a design decision changes a character's arc, personality, or role. Claude reads this before making changes to NPC code or story text.

## Kenji (健二) — Salaryman, 34

**Who he is:** A Shinjuku office worker drowning in corporate Japan. Works 12-hour days, eats at his desk, hasn't taken vacation in two years. Outwardly composed — suit always pressed, bows always correct. Inwardly tired in a way he can't name.

**How you meet:** He nearly knocks your tracts to the ground at the station. He stops. Reads the title. Hands you his business card before hurrying off. That's more than most people do.

**Personality:** Formal, guarded, private. He respects effort and patience. He doesn't open up easily — but when he does, he means it. He's not hostile to Christianity; he just doesn't know what to do with it. His father was absent. He's never seen what faith looks like in a real person.

**How language changes him:**
- Level 0: Phone translator between you. Long silences. He stays anyway — that itself means something.
- Level 1: Basic exchange, small questions. He loosens slightly. You learn the surface things.
- Level 2+: Real conversation. He starts asking *you* things. The guard comes down in pieces.

**Relationship arc:**
- Stranger → Acquaintance: Brief station meetups, polite distance, slow curiosity
- Acquaintance → Friend: Corporate stress surfaces. He says things he'd never say at work.
- Friend → Open: Questions about faith, doubt, his father. He's never spoken this way to anyone.
- Open → Studying: He wants to understand — intellectually first, then personally
- Studying → Believer: The Gospel becomes personal, not philosophical
- Believer → Disciple (D1, v35): He reads on his commute. He asks practical questions about prayer. He looks for fathers in church history.
- Disciple → Servant (D1, v35): He stays after to clean up. He invites a coworker. He starts taking responsibility before being asked.
- Servant → Elder (D1, v35 / unlocked by D3): He drafts the hospitality rota. He tracks who needs a call this week. He apologizes for being so "salaryman" about it — and the flock is cared for.

**Where you meet:** Station (early), Café (later)

**Church role:** **Elder, organizational gifting.** He's organized, respected, and when he commits to something he means it. In Phase D2 his passive effect will be income (his salaryman tithe) + coworker introductions — the gifting expressed mechanically.

**Language sensitivity:** High (langWeight: 1.0) — verbal communication is the relationship. Without language, you're just two people sitting near each other.

**Design notes:**
- His mother prayed at the household altar every morning for a husband who had already emotionally left
- He has a younger sister he cares about but rarely sees
- He said once: "Onsen are the church of Japanese people." He meant it as a joke. It wasn't entirely.

## Yuki (由紀) — University student, 21

**Who she is:** Linguistics major at a Tokyo university. Thinks in structures and patterns — she analyzes language the way some people analyze music. Came to the English conversation event to study natural speech. Stayed because something felt different about you.

**How you meet:** She's the last one to leave after the English event, notebook out. "Your English feels more human than textbooks," she says. You exchange LINE IDs before you finish putting the chairs away.

**Personality:** Intellectually fearless, emotionally cautious. She'll ask any question. She'll resist any answer that feels imposed. She's not cynical — she's careful. Faith to her is a serious thing, which is why she hasn't dismissed it.

**How language changes her:**
- Level 0: She leads in English. The dynamic is slightly off — she's teaching you, not the other way. She finds this interesting.
- Level 1: More balanced. Your Japanese impresses her. Language is her world; effort in it earns real respect.
- Level 2+: She code-switches with you, Japanese and English in the same sentence. The relationship feels like a meeting of equals.

**Relationship arc:**
- Stranger → Acquaintance: She comes back with a list of written questions. Theology as linguistics puzzle.
- Acquaintance → Friend: She admits she's been visiting a church near her apartment, "just to see"
- Friend → Open: Her grandmother's incense rituals. What does faith mean when family is woven into other beliefs?
- Open → Studying: Gospel of John. She reads it in Japanese and English side by side.
- Studying → Believer: She understands the Incarnation in a way that undoes her. "God couldn't just send a message."
- Believer → Disciple (D1, v35): She brings two Bibles. She reads Romans on her own. She makes peace with her grandmother's butsudan as memory, not worship.
- Disciple → Servant (D1, v35): She starts informally teaching a classmate. She prepares notes "in case someone needs them."
- Servant → Elder (D1, v35 / unlocked by D3): She teaches half the Sunday study. She is clear, slow, kind. She reaches people you cannot.

**Where you meet:** Café (always — it's her territory)

**Church role:** **Elder, teaching gifting.** Her linguistic precision and intellectual depth make her exceptional at explaining faith. In Phase D2 her passive effect will be wisdom shared with you + silent discipling of other believers (she helps Hiro through Romans on her own time) — the teaching gifting expressed mechanically.

**Language sensitivity:** High (langWeight: 1.0) — language is her discipline. Your effort with Japanese is itself a signal about who you are.

**Design notes:**
- She's an only child. Her grandmother's faith is important family history, not something she can dismiss
- She brings a friend to the English event once "just to see" — that's how it starts with others too
- She cried once during a conversation about family. She wasn't embarrassed about it.

## Hiro (浩) — Retired, 68

**Who he is:** A retired civil servant who comes to Yoyogi Park every morning to feed the pigeons. His wife Emiko died last year. He doesn't know what to do with the time she used to fill.

**How you meet:** He watches you preach from his bench. When you sit beside him, he doesn't leave. After a long silence, he says something about his wife. He doesn't explain. He doesn't have to.

**Personality:** Quiet, still, unhurried. He's past the age where pretense feels worth the effort. He says what he means in few words. He's not sad exactly — he's in a long aftermath, learning what remains when everything expected has already happened.

**How language changes him:**
- Level 0: Mostly silence, shared coffee from the vending machine, watching pigeons. The relationship is real. It doesn't need words.
- Level 1: He speaks slowly and simply — he's patient with your Japanese. Short true things.
- Level 2+: He talks about Emiko. About what she believed. About the Bible in the bedside drawer.

**Relationship arc:**
- Stranger → Acquaintance: Presence. You show up. He doesn't leave. That's all.
- Acquaintance → Friend: He tells you Emiko's name. He tells you she prayed.
- Friend → Open: He asks if the dead can hear us. He's not asking philosophically.
- Open → Studying: He reads Emiko's Bible. Her bookmarks are still inside.
- Studying → Believer: He asks you to pray out loud. He closes his eyes. "I think she prayed like that."
- Believer → Disciple (D1, v35): He reads in Emiko's chair at home now. Some days he doesn't come to the bench. White chrysanthemums sometimes appear on the bench when he does.
- Disciple → Servant (D1, v35): He visits a widow from his building. He doesn't preach. He sits. "I know that bench." He's noticing people.
- Servant → Elder (D1, v35 / unlocked by D3): He's the one who notices when someone has stopped coming. He brings tea. He sits on their bench. You did not appoint him. Grace did.

**Where you meet:** Park (always — the bench is his whole world right now; later, also at the apartment for studies)

**Church role:** **Elder, pastoral gifting.** He remembers everyone's names, their children's names, what people are carrying. In Phase D2 his passive effect will be mood lift on all met NPCs + stress reduction on overwhelmed ones — the long aftermath of grief turned into shepherding instinct, expressed mechanically.

**Language sensitivity:** Low (langWeight: 0.3) — presence and silence are the relationship. You don't need fluency to sit with someone in grief.

**Design notes:**
- His wife was named Emiko. She liked chrysanthemums. She got up before dawn to pray.
- He comes to the park every day, faces the same direction
- He said "Emiko would have liked you." That was the moment.
- He started reading her Bible on his own, before you asked him to.
